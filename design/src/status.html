Status is one vocabulary, said three ways

A dot carries the state everywhere it appears. The vitals bar says the same states as counts, and a project tally repeats them with the dot beside the number, so a row of the fleet list and the header never disagree about what a session is. What none of those three can do is say what the words mean — parked is aivis's own invention and idle sounds like a criticism when it only means a terminal is open — so the same sentence sits behind every count as a tooltip and, spelled out, in the legend below.

Every session is in exactly one of five states, decided by whether a claude process is alive and by what its transcript last recorded. asking you and waiting on you are not states but queues, drawn from those sessions. Right now 18 sessions have a live process on this machine.

working
A claude process is alive and its transcript is advancing right now. This is the only state where tokens are being spent.
idle
A process is alive but its turn has finished: a terminal you left open. It costs nothing, holds its whole context, and carries on the moment you write to it.
asking you
The session put a multiple-choice question to you and stopped mid-turn for the answer. This is the only queue here that a session asked to be in rather than being inferred from silence, and the only one where something is genuinely blocked. A session aivis drives can be answered from its own page; one running in a terminal has to be answered there.
waiting on you
An idle session that stopped within the last 4 hours, so it is probably holding for your reply rather than simply left open. These are counted under idle as well — the queue is a view of the fleet, not a place a session moves to.
stalled
Alive and mid-turn, but nothing has been written for over 2 minutes and it has not said why. Usually a long-running command, or a session in a terminal holding at a prompt only that terminal can answer. A session that stopped to ask you something is counted under asking you instead, because it said so.
parked
No process is running, but aivis saw this session alive before — usually the machine was shut down. Sending to it resumes it exactly where it stopped. Kept for 14 days after it was last seen running.
ended
No process and no record of one: an ordinary finished transcript. Still on disk, and still resumable by copying its resume command into a terminal.
